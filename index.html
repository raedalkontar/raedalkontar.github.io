Raed Al Kontar

I lead a data science group in the Industrial & Operations Engineering (IOE) department at the University of Michigan (UMich).

I work on developing data analytics methods for solving engineering problems, and I care about both the theory behind them and their use in practice. My focus is on personalized and distributed data analytics, where knowledge from diverse data sources is effectively integrated. This allows models to remain tailored to individual sources, inference to be distributed or decentralized, and personal data to be protected when needed. I have deployed these methods on manufacturing systems and applied them in healthcare.

More recently, I have started to explore language as an additional modality for improving decision-making, and to bring quality and reliability thinking to AI so that AI systems can be audited and trusted.

Featured papers

Language-Induced Priors for Domain Adaptation

Qiyuan Chen, Jiayu Zhou, Raed Kontar (corresponding author) · NeurIPS · 2026

When a new system has almost no data, statistical methods cannot tell which related sources are worth borrowing from, and borrowing from the wrong ones does harm. Yet text describing the new system, such as maintenance logs or operator notes, is often available and ignored. We turn it into a prior over the sources, learned from a large language model, and fold it into an EM algorithm: the prior guides the choice of sources when data are scarce, and the data take over as they accumulate.

Personalized PCA: Decoupling Shared and Unique Features

Naichen Shi, Raed Kontar (corresponding author) · Journal of Machine Learning Research · 2024

We decompose heterogeneous datasets into global principal components shared by all and local components unique to each, enforcing orthogonality so the two span different subspaces. Unlike traditional PCA such a model is not always identifiable; we give an identifiability condition based on misalignment of local components, and show the estimation error shrinks as heterogeneity grows. Heterogeneity turns out to be a blessing in disguise.

Collaborative and Distributed Bayesian Optimization via Consensus

Xubo Yue, Yang Liu, Albert S. Berahas, Blake N. Johnson, Raed Kontar (corresponding author) · IEEE Transactions on Automation Science and Engineering · 2025

Black-box optimization demands extensive trial and error, especially for systems with many design parameters. We introduce a collaborative framework that dynamically distributes that effort across potentially heterogeneous clients, bringing the notion of consensus to optimal design. It closes with a real experiment in which collaborating chemists optimize the formulation of a hydrogel.
